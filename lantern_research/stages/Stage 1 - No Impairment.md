# Stage 1 — No Impairment

**GDS Classification:** Stage 1 — No Cognitive Decline
**Alternate Names:** Pre-clinical Alzheimer's, asymptomatic phase
**Duration:** Indefinite; may persist for life if pathology never develops
**Care Dependency:** None

---

## Clinical Snapshot

At this stage, the person functions normally in every domain — work, social life, daily activities, independent living. There are no subjective complaints and no objective signs of cognitive decline.

**What's happening in the brain:** Pathological changes (amyloid plaque accumulation, tau protein tangles) may be occurring silently, years or decades before any symptoms appear. This is detectable only through advanced imaging (PET scans, spinal fluid analysis) in a research setting.

**Important truth:** Most people at Stage 1 will never develop Alzheimer's. The presence of brain pathology does not guarantee clinical decline — some brains are resilient enough to compensate.

---

## What You'll See

- Normal memory function
- Normal judgment and decision-making
- Normal social and occupational functioning
- No concerns raised by the person, family, or coworkers

**The risk here is not what you see — it's what you don't see.** Stage 1 is not a caregiving stage. It is a **preparation window**.

---

## What to Do at This Stage

If you have a family history of Alzheimer's or dementia:

**For the person who may eventually need care:**
- Have the conversation about future wishes while cognition is fully intact
- Encourage a brain-healthy lifestyle (the "MIND" diet, regular exercise, cognitive engagement, social connection, sleep hygiene, blood pressure control)
- Consider documenting baseline cognitive function with a primary care provider

**For the potential caregiver:**
- Educate yourself about the disease — you're doing that right now
- Build a support network before you need one
- Understand your family's medical and financial landscape

**This is the only stage where advance care planning can be done without the weight of cognitive decline.** Use it wisely.

---

## Preparation for the Road Ahead

Stage 1 is the ideal time to lay groundwork:

1. **Learn the warning signs** of Stage 2 (subjective memory complaints) so you recognize the transition
2. **Normalize the conversation** about aging and cognition in your family — reduce stigma before it's needed
3. **Know your resources** — local Alzheimer's Association chapter, geriatric care managers, elder law attorneys — before you need to find them in a crisis
