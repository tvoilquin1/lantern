# The Stagewise Dementia Care Framework

**Project codename:** Lantern — *light for the road ahead*

A structured synthesis of the Global Deterioration Scale (7 Stages of Alzheimer's) and the caregiving wisdom consolidated in *The 36-Hour Day*, translated into a practical, stage-by-stage guide for dementia caregivers.

## How to Use This Framework

This vault is organized so you can read it linearly (start to finish) or jump to the stage that describes your current situation.

Each stage note follows a consistent template:
- **Clinical Snapshot** — what's happening, in plain language
- **What You'll See** — cognitive, behavioral, and physical signs
- **Daily Care Guide** — practical how-tos for hygiene, safety, nutrition, communication
- **Caregiver Preparation & Coping** — emotional readiness, transition warnings, self-care
- **Resources & References** — where to go deeper

Cross-cutting reference notes cover topics that span multiple stages, like legal planning, communication, and end-of-life care.

## Key Principles (Across All Stages)

### 1. The Person Is Still a Person
Dementia does not erase identity, feelings, or need for connection. The person you care for is still there, even when they cannot express themselves. Assume they understand more than they can show.

### 2. Stage Transition Is the Most Vulnerable Time
The hardest moments are not *within* a stage, but *between* them — when the previous coping strategies stop working and the new reality hasn't yet been accepted. The framework flags these transition signals at the end of each stage.

### 3. Caregiver Survival Is Not Optional
You cannot pour from an empty cup. Each stage has specific burnout risks. The framework names them and gives you permission to ask for help, to rest, and to protect your own health — not as an afterthought, but as a core part of the care plan.

### 4. Adapt the Environment, Not the Person
The person with dementia cannot adapt to complexity, confusion, or noise. The caregiver's job is to reshape the environment — physical, social, emotional — to match the person's current abilities.

### 5. Plan Ahead While Clarity Remains
Financial, legal, and care decisions are far easier to make in Stages 2–3 than in Stages 5–6. The framework includes a [[Legal & Financial Planning Timeline]] that tells you what to do and when.
